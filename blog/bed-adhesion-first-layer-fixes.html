Bed Adhesion: Fixing the First Layer for Good

2026-09-045 min read

When a print fails at minute three, the diagnosis is almost always the same: the first layer did not bond. The culprit list is short, and so are the fixes.

The four causes, in order of likelihood

  • Dirty plate. Fingerprints are invisible release agents. Wash the plate with dish soap and hot water, dry with a clean towel — IPA alone smears grease around.
  • Wrong Z-offset. Too high and the line sits on the bed like spaghetti; too low and the nozzle plows furrows. Aim for lines that touch each other and look slightly squashed.
  • Unlevel bed. Re-level after any bump, blade scrape or nozzle change. Paper method or automatic — both are fine if done calmly.
  • Cold material. A first layer at 20–25 mm/s bonds far better than the same line at speed. Many profiles ship too fast.

Mechanical help when the basics are covered

A 5–8 mm brim rescues tall, thin parts like tree silhouettes and light stands. Rafts are the last resort — they waste material and leave the ugliest underside. Textured PEI plates barely need help at all; smooth plates like a whiff of glue stick for stubborn filaments.

Practice on cheap, flat, forgiving models — the keychain collection is ideal. Once the first layer is boring, articulated toys become routine too, because print-in-place lives and dies by that same layer.
